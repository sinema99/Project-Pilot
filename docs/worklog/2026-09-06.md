# Worklog — 2026-09-06

Two sessions, one thread: **pilot9 on Real Controller**. Built 2026-09-05 (retarget, scene,
controller, trial), extended 2026-09-06 (crouch, materials, re-export). Spec was written first
and agreed before any of it — [specs/pilot9-real-controller.md](../specs/pilot9-real-controller.md)

that neither of those files would have predicted.

Everything below shipped in `ce2aa1a`, the first commit since the 09-04 reduction.

## The premise, restated

Setsuna's locomotion is hand-animated, and the animation supply — not the movement code — is the
bottleneck. pilot9 is the bet that a rig on Godot's humanoid profile turns "new move" into a
Mixamo download instead of a Blender session. Nothing of Setsuna's was deleted; her fate waits on
the user driving this.

## The retarget

**The one finding that cost real time: the BoneMap key.** RC's own `character.fbx.import` uses
`PATH:Skeleton3D`. pilot9's glb puts the skeleton under an `Armature` node, so the key has to be
`PATH:Armature/Skeleton3D`. With the wrong key **the import runs, reports nothing, and simply
does not retarget** — no error, no warning, no dialog. Same class of failure as everything else
on this rig: silence. Map targets are the underscore names (`mixamorig_Hips`), Godot 4.6's
`naming_version=2` form, not the colon form the Blender export shows.

What landed once the key was right: skeleton renamed `GeneralSkeleton` (which is exactly what
RC's track paths address, and the whole reason the profile route was chosen), 22 mapped bones
renamed to profile names, `motion_scale` normalised to ~1.06, and the `Armature` node's `0.01`
scale + 90° X rotation baked out to identity.

**Two of the spec's five risks were wrong, and in the good direction.**

- **Risk 2 (height).** RC's `motion_scale` 0.9797 against pilot9's 1.06 was expected to need "a
  tuning pass, not a first-try fit". Position tracks are scaled by `motion_scale`, so it resolved
  itself. The capsule was measured from the retargeted mesh at **1.9552 m** (RC's own is ~1.75)
  and has not moved since.
- **Risk 5 (the outline scribble).** The spec asserted Setsuna's "every shell 1.0-weighted to a
  single bone" rule for pilot9 too. **It does not apply to him.** He came off Mixamo's
  auto-rigger, which paints smooth weights: 40 of his 41 shells span two or more bones, he has
  now animated under the cel material, and the outline does not scribble. That rule is Setsuna's
  alone, and `check_weights.py` is therefore **not a gate** — it is for spotting an *unintended*
  weight change between exports. Compare against the previous run, not against zero.

Risk 3 (missing bones) played out as written: finger, eye, jaw and `Root` tracks do not resolve
and the test distinguishes those known-absent bones from a genuine failure.

## Three deviations from stock RC

The spec said "stock Real Controller feel", and it held — no dash, no slide, no double jump, no
apex-hang bands, no slope pitch. But three things had to change, each marked at its site in
`pilot9.gd`:

1. **Every `Input.mouse_mode` write removed.** Cursor state is owned project-wide by
   `scripts/ui/ui_manager.gd`; RC grabbing the mouse on `_ready`, on click and on `_exit_tree`
   fought it. The ESC and left-click handlers existed *only* to toggle capture, so they went with
   it. The mouse-look maths they wrapped stayed.
2. **Controller pitch sign.** Stock adds where its own mouse path subtracts — the right stick
   came out inverted against the mouse. Now subtracted.
3. **Character rotation gated on movement input.** Stock welds the body to camera-back every
   frame, so orbiting the camera just dragged pilot9 round with it and his face was unreachable.
   Standing still he now holds his world facing; the frame input resumes he re-welds at
   `rotation_speed`. That re-weld is what keeps backing up and strafing reading against the
   camera rather than against wherever he was left pointing.

## The scene is generated — that is the design, not a shortcut

`scenes/pilot9.tscn` bakes the rig in rather than instancing the glb, and inlines the mesh, skin
and materials. Both are forced:

- RC's clips address the skeleton as `%GeneralSkeleton`, a **scene-unique name**, which only
  resolves if the skeleton node is *owned by* `pilot9.tscn`.
- An `ExtResource` into `res://.godot/imported/pilot9.glb-<hash>.scn` breaks the moment the hash
  changes on the next reimport.

The consequence is the thing to remember: **a Blender change does not reach the game until the
builder re-runs.** Reimporting alone leaves the old rig in the scene and the export looks like it
failed.

That is also why the builder has two modes. `swap` replaces only `character/Armature` and
preserves what was tuned in the editor; `--fresh` rebuilds from RC's `character.tscn` and
discards it. So anything that must survive a `--fresh` — the GLB clips, the crouch state — lives
in the builder, not in the editor. The builder prints measured mesh height against the capsule's
and says so when they drift, but **never resizes the capsule in swap mode**, so a re-export
cannot silently undo a collider tuned by hand.

The AnimationTree is the part worth all this care: a state machine wrapping 9 `BlendSpace2D`s and
78 animation nodes with hand-placed blend points. The build swaps the model *underneath* it.

## The crouch (2026-09-06)

Not in the spec — the spec explicitly deferred crouch to "when the Mixamo animations land". One
landed: `P.crouchwalk`, authored in Blender and riding in on `pilot9.glb`, which is the cheap
route because the same BoneMap retargets it and its tracks come out addressing
`%GeneralSkeleton:<ProfileBone>` already. No `.res`, no second `.import`.

**Toggle, not hold** (C, and controller B). **A TimeScale, not a blend**, because pilot9 has a
crouch *walk* and nothing to stand still in: the state is the clip through an
`AnimationNodeTimeScale` and `_drive_crouch()` eases the scale to 0 when he is not moving, so he
holds the pose he stopped on instead of marching on the spot. A Mixamo "Crouch Idle" makes this a
real blend and the TimeScale can go the day it exists.

`crouch_speed` is **1.6 m/s**, matched by eye against the clip's own cadence — change one without
the other and he skates or moonwalks.

Jumping while crouched **stands him up and jumps**: there is no crouch-jump clip and a dead jump
key reads as broken input. `pilot9.gd` clears `is_crouching` and sets `velocity.y` on the same
frame, which makes `crouch -> jump` and `crouch -> Locomotion` both eligible on that frame — so
jump is priority 0 and the stand-up is 2, or the leap plays as a stand-up. `jump_land -> crouch`
exists for the same class of reason: `jump_land`'s only other exit needs `velocity.length() >
0.2`, so landing still while crouched would strand him standing.

### The bug that took the session: `deterministic` was off

Crouch once and pilot9 stood back up **hunched forward, permanently, for the life of the scene.**

Real Controller ships its AnimationTree with `deterministic` **off**, which is not Godot's
default. Off, a bone whose blend weight reaches zero — no playing clip animates it — keeps
whatever value it last held instead of returning to rest. That is harmless while every clip
animates the same bones, which was true of RC's own 27 and **stopped being true the moment a clip
arrived from a different source.** `crouch_walk` rotates `UpperChest` ~22°; no RC clip touches
that bone. Standing up blended the crouch out of every bone the two share and left that one
stranded at its crouched angle.

`_ensure_deterministic_blending()` turns it back on in both builder modes. The cost is real and
accepted: while crouched, bones the crouch clip does not animate — both wrists — sit at rest
rather than holding the pose idle left them in.

**Expect this from every future mixed-source clip.** It is not crouch-specific, and the test
asserts it as the invariant (a clip animating a bone the others ignore is normal and unavoidable
once clips come from two places) rather than as a flag.

One thing that will look alarming in a future scene diff: **`deterministic = true` is not written
into `pilot9.tscn` any more.** `true` is Godot 4.6's class default — RC's `character.tscn`
carries an explicit `deterministic = false`, which is what made this a bug in the first place —
and a property matching its class default is not serialised. The flag is still set on every
build and the test still holds it. Its absence from the `.tscn` text is correct.

## Findings worth keeping

**This rig fails silently, in three separate places, and they are all the same shape.**

| Seam | What a rename does | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| A wrong BoneMap key in `.import` | import runs, reports nothing, does not retarget | `test_import_file_declares_the_bone_map` |
| `advance_expression` naming a property (`is_crouching`) | the expression never fires, and never complains | `test_the_controller_exposes_is_crouching_and_crouch_speed` |
| `AnimationTree.set()` on a parameter path | stores it, no-op, not an error | the path is a `const` on both sides; `test_the_timescale_parameter_path_matches_what_the_feed_writes` |

And the one the whole retarget test file exists for: **a clip whose tracks resolve to nothing
plays without error.** No crash, no dialog — pilot9 stands in T-pose while the state machine
transitions happily around him. So the load-bearing assertion is that every track path in every
shipped clip lands on a real bone.

**The cursor bug was ours, and it was in `ui_manager`.** With RC's `mouse_mode` writes removed,
mouse look in the trial was simply dead on launch. `ui_manager` starts at `screen = NONE` and
only ever wrote `mouse_mode` inside `_dispatch` — so with no screen open at boot, nothing ever
captured the cursor and you had to open and close a menu to get camera control. It now applies
its opening state in `_ready` instead of assuming the engine already matches it. That is a
project-wide fix that only surfaced because pilot9 was the first scene to stop fighting it.

**RC ships 28 clips and its own tree uses 27.** `jump_begin` is referenced by nothing, in RC's
scene or ours. The spec's "30 clips" was a miscount off the README.

**Material slots crossed over for free.** The 09-06 export gained three (`main`, `secondary`,
`scarf`) where it previously had none, and `ApplyCelPilot9` picks them up with no wiring at all —
`derive_from_surfaces` reads each Blender slot name and its `albedo_color` and stamps a cel
material carrying that colour. The colour must cross **untouched**: the importer has already
sRGB-encoded glTF's linear `baseColorFactor` and the shader's `source_color` uniform expects
exactly that encoding. Check against the export's `baseColorFactor`, never by eye.

**Cel look is ON.** The spec had it start off, on the theory that the first animation of a
retargeted rig was also the first chance for the outline to scribble. It was flipped on 09-05
once the retarget checked out, and `test_trial_still_carries_the_cel_pass_on_pilot9` now holds it
there — a dropped target is how the outline silently stops applying.

## Tests

**161 tests / 655 checks across 10 files, green.** Three files are new:
`test_pilot9_retarget.gd` (11), `test_pilot9_crouch.gd` (16), `test_player_slope.gd` (8).

One of them was red on the first full run and is worth recording, because it was the *test*
that was right for the wrong reason. `test_trial_has_exactly_one_camera_and_it_is_the_rc_rig_camera`
asserts the rig `Camera3D` is marked `current`; RC ships it unset and the builder was not setting
it either. Nothing was visibly broken — Godot promotes the first `Camera3D` to enter a viewport
when no other is active, so the trial always rendered. It stops being invisible the day a scene
instancing pilot9 carries a camera of its own: which one wins is then decided by tree order, and
the loser is a black screen or a view from the wrong place. `_ensure_camera_current()` now sets
the flag in both builder modes, so it survives a `--fresh` where an editor tick would not.

`tests/run.ps1` had to be rewritten to reach a trustworthy verdict at all. `run_tests.gd` quits 0
on pass and 1 on fail, but Godot **segfaults during headless shutdown** (leaked Canvas RIDs) and
overwrites the code with `0xC0000005`, so `$LASTEXITCODE` reported failure on a green suite. The
runner now parses the printed `PASS`/`FAIL` summary line and treats a missing one as a failure —
the suite not finishing is itself a result. (PowerShell 5.1 wraps a native command's stderr in
`ErrorRecord`s, hence the `ToString()`.)

## Also in this commit — Setsuna's slope traversal

Not pilot9 work, but it shipped alongside and is the other half of `player.gd`'s day.

Walking a ramp read as skating across the level. Two changes, and the split between them is the
point: **`floor_snap_length` raised to 0.5 m** is the physical fix — the engine default of 0.1 m
is fine at a walk, but one tick at 9 m/s drops further than that off the top of a downgrade, so
she went briefly airborne every frame and the descent stuttered like stairs. **`_slope_pitch()`
is pure show** — it tips the visible model along the grade while the capsule that does the
colliding stays bolt upright, eased at the same speed as a turn so cresting a ramp sweeps rather
than snaps, with a 3° deadzone so collision-mesh noise on flat floor cannot make her twitch.

It is static and pure precisely because the sign convention is easy to get backwards, and a
flipped sign looks exactly like a model that rears back going downhill. Eight tests hold it.
Model pitch is also cleared on embark and disembark, alongside the run lift, for the same reason
that one already was: a freeze mid-stride leaves it on and the climb is not a slope.

## Known issues / next steps

- **The capsule does not shrink when crouching.** Crouching is a pose and a speed, not a
  duck-under-geometry mechanic, and nothing in `trial.tscn` is low enough to duck under. It
  belongs in the builder's `PILOT_HEIGHT` path when it is wanted — the builder deliberately never
  resizes the capsule in swap mode.
- **`crouch` and `ui_cancel` share controller B.** Harmless while the trial carries no UI. The
  day a pause menu lands there, back-out and crouch fire together and one has to move.
- **RC's speeds are stock** — 2.5 walk / 5.0 run / 8.0 sprint, against `player.gd`'s 6.0 / 9.0.
  If 5 m/s feels like wading, note the 8-direction blend spaces are authored against RC's speeds:
  retuning is its own pass, not a constant edit.
- **`sit_down` is imported and deliberately unwired.** It is the climb-into-the-cockpit clip for
  a mech handover that does not exist yet.
- **The licence question is still unread.** RC's clips come from Kevin Iglesias' *Basic Motions
  Free*. Commercial terms need settling before anything ships — not before more building.
- **AMS's `PoseWarping`** (slope warping, foot IK) is now liftable: pilot9's bones are on the
  profile, which was the thing blocking it. Squarely relevant to a slope-traversal game, and it
  would supersede the model-pitch trick above.
- **Setsuna's retirement is still undecided.** It waits on the user driving pilot9. Nothing of
  hers was touched in this pass beyond the slope work.
