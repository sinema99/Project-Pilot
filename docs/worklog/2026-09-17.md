# Worklog — 2026-09-17

**pilot9's dash.** Spec first — [specs/pilot9-dash.md](../specs/pilot9-dash.md) — then built,
then played the same day. This entry records the play-session retune: what the user asked to
change after trying it, and what actually moved in `scripts/pilot9.gd` and
`tests/test_pilot9_dash.gd`. The build itself (the vanish, the two limiters, the ink streak, the
`dash` input action, the `tab_abilities.gd` row) is not re-described here — the spec still holds
for all of it except the two points below.

## What the user said

"the dash looks really good" — the build itself was not in question. Then three asks: snappier
(speed up a little), go a little farther, and remove the shadow.

## What changed

**`dash_speed` 18.0 → 20.0 m/s.** Covers both "snappier" and "farther" at once: `dash_time`
stayed at 0.5 s (the user's own 2026-09-17 number, untouched), and since `dash_distance()` is
just `dash_speed * dash_time`, the one knob moved both feel and reach together rather than
tuning them as two separate levers.

**Distance moved from 9.0 m to 10.0 m as a side effect**, not a second deliberate change. The
spec's own "The 9.0 m collision" section had already flagged landing exactly on the sprint
jump's 9 m gap lip as the one value a measured-gap repo shouldn't want — the speed bump resolved
that by accident, in the direction the spec said was preferable. Recorded in the spec inline so
the next person tuning `dash_speed` knows 10.0 m is now load-bearing for the same reason 9.0 m
was flagged as a problem.

**The raking-shadow cue is cut.** `_hide_for_dash()` used to set every mesh under `character/`
to `cast_shadow = SHADOWS_ONLY`, so his shadow kept tearing across the floor while he was
invisible — the second of the two look cues the spec designed ("He goes shadows-only, not
invisible"). It now sets `mi.visible = false` instead: a clean vanish, no shadow. The ink streak
is the only cue left.

Likely why it read as unwanted rather than as reinforcement: the shadow cue only ever showed up
on a floor underfoot, so it was inconsistent by construction — present on a ground dash, absent
the moment he dashed over a gap, which is exactly where the streak is doing the most work
already. The spec's own "why the two cues pair" reasoning (bright background → streak carries
it, dark background → shadow carries it) is now moot; watch for streak legibility over dark
ground becoming a complaint with no second cue backstopping it, since that's the one blind spot
the shadow used to cover.

`_dash_shadow_cache` keeps its name but now stores `{"mesh", "visible"}` instead of
`{"mesh", "cast_shadow"}` — restore still returns each mesh to what it actually had before the
dash touched it, not a blanket `true`, the same rule the cast_shadow version followed.

## Tests

`tests/test_pilot9_dash.gd` updated in place: the two number assertions (18.0→20.0,
9.0→10.0 renamed to `test_dash_distance_is_ten_metres`), and the hide/restore round-trip
(`test_hide_reaches_invisible_and_restore_undoes_it_exactly`, checking `visible` instead of
`cast_shadow`). 527 tests / 2595 checks pass headless.

## What cannot be claimed

Feel, same as the spec already says. Whether 20.0 m/s reads as "snappier" or the streak alone
carries the vanish well enough without the shadow are both play-session questions, not
headless-test ones.
