## Hub shading pass — "the hub map is so dark"

Symptom: in `test.tscn` at runtime the hub reads as a dark muddy brown mass, exterior and
interior alike, while the same hub in the editor viewport reads as a bright, clean
blockout (neutral grey walls, blue-tinted floors, warm ceilings).

Cause, in the order it matters:

1. **The editor was not showing the stylized material at all.** In the editor the hub was
   still on its imported greybox `StandardMaterial3D` (0.45 grey), lit by the environment's
   **sky-sourced** ambient — which is what gives the blue-up / warm-down reading in the
   editor screenshot. At runtime `ApplyStylized` swaps in `stylized_material.tres`, whose
   `render_mode ambient_light_disabled` switches that sky ambient off and replaces it with
   the shader's own hemisphere at `ambient_energy = 0.35`. Much weaker fill.
2. **The shared material's albedo was the sand brown** `#956543`, not a neutral. Every hub
   surface inherited the terrain swatch.
3. **The darkest cel band returns literal 0.** The hub interior is enclosed, so the sun
   never reaches it: `ndotl * ATTENUATION = 0` everywhere inside → darkest band → lit by
   the weak hemisphere ambient only, multiplied by a dark brown albedo.

Fixes:

- `shaders/stylized.gdshader`: new **`shadow_floor`** uniform (ships at `0.0`). Remaps the
  banded output into `[shadow_floor, 1]` **after** the halftone branch, so the dotting
  still stipples between the shadow level and the band above it. Band edges stay hard.
- `resources/stylized_material.tres` (the hub + landmark material) retuned:
  albedo/gradient `#956543 → #A39C90` (warm neutral, near the editor greybox),
  `ambient_energy 0.35 → 1.1`, `shadow_floor 0 → 0.35`, `dot_density 0.55 → 0.3` (a whole
  interior of halftone reads as noise, not ink).
- `scenes/test.tscn`: the ground's inline `ShaderMaterial_sand` pins `shadow_floor = 0.0`
  explicitly, so the dune shadows keep the hard, dark contrast that already looked right.

Tuning knobs, in the order to reach for them: `ambient_energy` (overall interior level,
keeps the blue-floor / warm-ceiling split), then `shadow_floor` (flattens toward the sun
colour — too high and the cel bands collapse), then `albedo_color`.

Follow-up not done here: the hub interior still has **no light source of its own**. The
fill above is a stylistic cheat. Actual `OmniLight3D` / `SpotLight3D` placement inside the
blockout (already on this file's next-steps list) is the real fix and would let
`shadow_floor` come back down.

## Setsuna shading — shadow acne on the character

`assets/setsuna.glb` ships **no materials and no textures** (one mesh, 10 disconnected
boxes, 152 tris), so Godot gave her its default `StandardMaterial3D`: pure white,
`albedo = 1.0`. She was the only thing in `test.tscn` not on the stylized pipeline.

Two consequences, both visible:

- **Blown out.** White albedo × `light_energy 1.6` clips every sunlit face to solid white.
- **Shadow-map acne.** Nothing quantised the attenuation, so the shadow map's texel grid
  projected straight onto her as a fine 45° hatch. `shadow_normal_bias = 1.5` (authored
  against the hub blockout, and ~5× the width of her 0.31 m torso) papered over it at some
  angles and left a ragged, stair-stepped terminator at others. Verified by capture:
  dropping the normal bias to 0.05 made the hatch cover her entirely; disabling the sun's
  shadow removed it completely. Raising `shadow_bias` to 0.12 barely touched it.

Fix — put her on the stylized shader. The cel bands quantise the noisy attenuation, so the
acne lands inside a band and disappears; only the band edges are visible, and those are
geometry-aligned. Confirmed at `shadow_normal_bias = 0.05` (worst case): clean.

- **`resources/character_material.tres`** — her own swatch, deliberately not the hub's:
  light warm off-white `#E0DCD4`, `ambient_energy 0.7`, `shadow_floor 0.12` (she wants
  real form, unlike the flat interior), `dot_scale_px 5.0` (finer halftone on a small
  subject).
- **`scenes/test.tscn`** — second applier node `ApplyStylizedCharacter` pointed at
  `SETSUNA/PlayerModel`. Sun `shadow_bias 0.03 → 0.05`, `shadow_normal_bias 1.5 → 0.2`,
  now that nothing needs the oversized offset to hide acne. Hub look unchanged.

Also confirmed while looking: the **coloured slots at her neck, waist, elbows and knees are
background showing through**, not shading. The 10 box shells never touch — 29 mm at the
neck, 43 mm at the hips. Close the gaps in Blender (or accept them as a design choice).

## Setsuna scribble, part 2 — skin weights, not UVs, not shadows

The residual scribble on the torso and head after the material fix is a **different cause**
from the shadow acne above. Isolated by capture, one variable at a time:

| Variable | Result |
|---|---|
| Animation stopped (rest pose) | clean — this is why it never reproduced while frozen |
| `RUN LOOP` playing | scribble on torso + head, limbs clean |
| Sun `shadow_enabled = false` | **still there** — not shadow acne |
| Halftone `dot_enabled = false` | **still there** — not the dotting |
| Compositor detached | **clean** |
| `normal_edge_threshold` raised out of range | **clean** |
| `depth_edge_threshold` raised out of range | still there |

So: the compositor's **normal**-edge Sobel is firing across the whole surface of the
skinned boxes. Cause, read straight out of the GLB:

```
influences per vertex: {1: 232, 2: 33, 3: 39}
head/torso verts: spine.003 / spine.004 / spine.006 at e.g. (0.434, 0.382, 0.184)
```

232 of 304 verts are rigid to one bone — those are the limbs, and they render clean. The
72 multi-weighted verts are exactly the head and torso boxes. Each corner of those boxes
blends a *different* mix of three spine bones, so when the spine bends the corner normals
fan apart and get interpolated across what should be a flat face. The normal buffer then
has a gradient everywhere on that face and the outline pass inks all of it.

**Fix belongs in Blender, on the weights** (UVs are irrelevant — nothing in this pipeline
samples a texture or a UV; the halftone is `FRAGCOORD`-based). A blocky blockout wants
rigid weights: each box 1.0 to a single bone. Fastest route is Weight Paint →
*Weights → Limit Total* with Limit = 1, then *Normalize All*, then re-export.

Engine-side mitigation exists but is not worth taking: raising `normal_edge_threshold`
above ~1.0 kills the artifact and also kills the hub's crease lines.

### Resolved

Re-export (21:14) checked per shell: **all 10 shells rigid to exactly one bone** — head
`spine.006`, torso `spine.002`, upper arms on their own bones, limbs unchanged. Geometry
identical (304/304 verts). Verified in-engine with `RUN LOOP`, `IDLE` and `JUMP` playing:
flat faces, clean outlines, no scribble anywhere. The compositor's normal-edge pass has
nothing spurious to ink now.

Note the earlier `Limit Total = 1` attempt did not fix it: that operates per vertex, so the
boxes stayed torn between bones. The working fix was assigning each box wholly to one
vertex group at weight 1.0.

Export now also carries `SPRINT LOOP` and `DASH`. Neither is wired into `player.gd` yet —
they import fine and are inert until asked for.
