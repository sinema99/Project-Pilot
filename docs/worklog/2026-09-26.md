# Worklog — 2026-09-26

**pilot9's dash FOV kick — specced, built and played the same day.** See
[specs/pilot9-dash-fov.md](../specs/pilot9-dash-fov.md).

## What the user said

The ask: "a quick fov increase when pilot dashes ... nothing snappy and jarring but something
smooth. something that makes you feel like you're moving faster than you are." Both ground and
air dashes get it — the user's call.

After the play: "played it, feels good." No adjustments.

## What shipped

A dash widens the lens by `dash_fov_boost` (**+10°**) on top of the sprint FOV: eased open over
`dash_fov_rise_time` (**0.12 s**), held for the rest of the dash, then eased shut over
`dash_fov_release_time` (**0.6 s**) after it ends — well past the dash itself, which is the
"faster than you are" read. Both halves are `smoothstep`, so the close is gentle on the frame he
reappears rather than steepest there. The kick alone is capped at `dash_fov_ceiling` (**115°**)
so a high FOV setting plus sprint plus dash cannot go fisheye. CAMERA MOTION off holds it at
zero.

At the default FOV a sprint-dash peaks at 95° and settles back to the sprint's 85°.

## The one structural change

The sprint FOV used to lerp `camera_3d.fov` in place, reading it back each frame. Its eased
value now lives in `_sprint_fov`, and the camera is written as `_sprint_fov` plus the kick —
otherwise the sprint lerp would have dragged the kick down at its own rate. Sprint behaviour is
unchanged; `tests/test_pilot9_sprint_fov.gd` passes unedited.

## Decided, and held up in play

- **Not synced to the dash camera lag.** The lag is done at 0.35 s, the kick's close runs to
  0.85 s. Flagged as the risk to judge — it read as speed carrying, not as two effects.
- **+10° at a sprint-dash (95°)** — flagged as possibly a warp rather than a push. It wasn't.
